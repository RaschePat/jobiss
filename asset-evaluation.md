# Art Direction & Asset Evaluation v0.1

## Core Question
Does the finished page establish a distinctive, coherent visual world that strengthens Jobiss, or is it still a familiar AI frontend with surface-level styling?

## 1. Product Communication
- Is the core Jobiss value understandable?
- Is the primary action clear enough to remain viable?
- Do visual choices support rather than obscure the product story?

## 2. Art Direction
- Visual Thesis: is a clear visual argument perceptible without explanation?
- Composition: are scale, balance, density, whitespace, overlap, and eye flow deliberate?
- Typography: does type contribute to identity and rhythm rather than only hierarchy?
- Coherence: do UI, imagery, color, type, motion, and background belong to one world?
- Distinctiveness: would the page remain recognizable if generic branding were removed?
- Emotional Effect: does the page create the intended feeling?

## 3. Asset Quality
If bespoke/generated assets are used:
- are they necessary to the composition?
- do they reinforce product meaning?
- do they share one visual language?
- are crop, resolution, scale, and placement convincing?
- do they avoid generic AI illustration or stock-image aesthetics?

If no generated assets are used, do not penalize that choice when the Art Direction is stronger without them.

## 4. Aesthetic Ceiling Check
Ask explicitly:
- Does this feel like a competent template or like authored art direction?
- Are the memorable choices structural, or mostly decoration?
- Did the result escape predictable AI landing-page patterns?
- Is there at least one visual decision that feels specific to this product and difficult to substitute with another SaaS brand?

## 5. Diagnose Before Revising
Record the three highest-impact problems. Prefer revising composition, typography, imagery, or visual thesis before adding more ornament.

## Pass Condition
Pass only when Product Communication remains viable and the page demonstrates a coherent Visual Thesis with meaningful product-specific distinctiveness. A technically polished but generic frontend is `Needs Revision`.
