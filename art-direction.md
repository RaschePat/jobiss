# Art Direction Protocol v0.1

## Purpose
Raise the aesthetic ambition beyond a competent generic AI frontend by establishing a project-specific visual world before implementation.

## 1. Read the Product Meaning
Translate the product context into emotional and visual tensions. Identify what the service should feel like, not only what functions it contains.

## 2. Define an Aesthetic Ambition
Before coding, establish internally:
- desired emotional impression,
- level of visual tension,
- utility ↔ expression priority,
- what should feel familiar,
- what should feel unexpected,
- what must make this page recognizable as Jobiss rather than a generic career SaaS.

Do not use `clean, modern, professional` as the complete direction.

## 3. Reference Deconstruction
When browsing/reference tools are available and references would materially help, inspect strong contemporary editorial, digital-product, identity, or portfolio work. Do not copy a layout.

Extract reusable principles such as:
- composition logic,
- typography behavior,
- image-to-type relationship,
- scale contrast,
- rhythm and density,
- framing/cropping,
- texture/materiality,
- transition between sections,
- one memorable visual device.

## 4. Commit to One Visual Thesis
Write one internal sentence describing the visual argument. It must connect Jobiss's product meaning to visible design choices.

## 5. Design a World, Not a Component Collection
The page should feel governed by one visual system. Avoid solving every section independently with another card, pill, icon row, glow, or rounded container.

Consider typography, whitespace, image assets, composition, background, motion, and UI objects as parts of the same art direction.

## 6. Preserve Product Clarity
Art direction may introduce asymmetry, tension, unusual scale, overlap, controlled density, or deliberate friction, but the core product value and primary action must remain understandable.

## 7. Render and Reconsider
After implementation, inspect the rendered page and ask:
- Does the intended visual thesis actually appear?
- Does it still look like a familiar AI-generated frontend with a different skin?
- Which element is genuinely memorable?
- Would removing the logo make the design indistinguishable from another AI/SaaS landing page?

If the result remains generic and the issue is fixable, revise the highest-impact visual decision rather than adding decoration.
