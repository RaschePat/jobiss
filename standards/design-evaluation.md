# Design Evaluation v0.1

## Governing Question
Does this design accomplish what it is trying to accomplish?

Evaluate against the design intent defined before implementation. Do not reward a design merely for being conventional, simple, symmetrical, or easy.

## Layer 1 — Functional Quality
Evaluate:
- Information Architecture: can the user understand the page structure and priority?
- Legibility: is important content readable with appropriate hierarchy?
- Interaction Clarity: are primary actions and interactive states understandable?
- Accessibility / Robustness: are there obvious contrast, responsiveness, overflow, or interaction failures?

Question: **Does it work?**

## Layer 2 — Aesthetic Quality
Evaluate:
- Composition: proportion, balance, contrast, density, whitespace, and eye flow
- Visual Coherence: typography, form, color, imagery, spacing, and motion share a coherent language
- Distinctiveness: the result feels specific to this product rather than assembled from generic UI conventions
- Emotional Effect: visual choices create the intended mood
- Craft / Polish: details, alignment, states, rhythm, and finishing quality feel intentional

Question: **Is the visual result compelling and well made on its own terms?**

## Layer 3 — Intent Alignment
Evaluate:
- Brand Fit
- Audience Fit
- Desired Experience
- Visual Thesis Alignment
- Intentional Deviation

Question: **Does the reason for the design show in the result?**

## Intentional Deviation Test
When a design breaks a common convention, do not penalize it automatically. Ask:
1. Was the deviation intentional and connected to the pre-defined design intent?
2. What experience, meaning, attention, emotion, or distinction is gained?
3. Is that effect actually visible in the rendered result?
4. What usability/accessibility cost does it create?
5. Does the benefit outweigh the cost while preserving the core task?

If yes, preserve it. If no, revise it.

## Critical Failures
Treat these as revision-required regardless of aesthetic ambition:
- Core information or primary action is unusable
- Required project context is contradicted
- The visual thesis and actual result substantially conflict
- Severe unintended readability, layout, responsive, or interaction failure

## Evaluation Output
For internal evaluation, record:
- Functional: Strong / Mixed / Weak
- Aesthetic: Strong / Mixed / Weak
- Intent Alignment: Strong / Mixed / Weak
- Intentional deviations worth preserving
- Top 3 issues
- Highest-impact revision direction
- Pass / Needs Revision

For controlled experiments, optional 1–5 sub-scores may be recorded as comparison data, but a numerical average must not replace the qualitative judgment.
